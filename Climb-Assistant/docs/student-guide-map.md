# Student Guide — The MAP Stage

MAP is the first stage of your Climb. Before you look anything up or talk to the AI tutor, you stop and think about what you already know — and what you need to find out. The idea is to go into your research with a plan, not just a search bar.

There are five things to complete in MAP. You can see your progress on the checklist at the top of the page: **Understand → Recall → Identify → Question → Plan.**

---

## 1. Understand the task

**What it's asking:** Write the task in your own words. What are you actually being asked to do? What kind of response is expected — an explanation, an argument, an analysis?

This isn't about answering the question yet. It's about making sure you've properly read it and understood what's needed before you begin.

> *Tip: If you can't explain the task in your own words, you're not ready to research it yet. Re-read the task brief by clicking View all in the header.*

---

## 2. Recall — what you already know

**What it's asking:** List the things you already know that are relevant to this task.

Start every point with "I know that…" You need at least two points to tick this off, but the more you can recall, the better your TORCH conversation will be — the AI tutor uses your prior knowledge to push your thinking further.

> *Tip: Don't worry about being right. Write what you think you know. You might find some of it needs correcting later — that's the point.*

---

## 3. Identify — your knowledge gaps

**What it's asking:** List the things you don't know yet, or the parts of the task you're not confident about.

Start each one with "I need to understand…" or "I'm not sure about…" You need at least three gaps to tick this off.

> *Tip: The better your gaps, the better your research. Vague gaps ("I don't know much about it") won't help you. Specific gaps ("I need to understand why the rate of photosynthesis increases with light intensity up to a point") will.*

---

## 4. Question — your enquiry questions

**What it's asking:** Write the smaller questions you'll need to answer before you can complete the task. These break your big task question into manageable steps.

You need at least three enquiry questions. You can add as many as you like.

> *Tip: Good enquiry questions aren't just "What is X?" They're questions that require you to think — "Why does X happen?", "How does X affect Y?", "To what extent is X a cause of Y?"*

---

## 5. Plan — your research and verification plan

**What it's asking:** For each thing you need to investigate, write down how you'll verify what you find. Anyone can find information — the skill is knowing whether to trust it.

For each row:
- **Investigate** — what are you going to look into?
- **Sources** — what kind of sources will you use? (pick from the suggestions or add your own)
- **Verify** — how will you check the information is reliable? (e.g. "Compare with an independent source", "Check the publication date", "Look for supporting evidence")

You can add more rows using the **+ Add row** button if you have multiple things to investigate.

---

## Finishing MAP

Once all five checklist items are ticked, you can click **View MAP Summary** to see everything you've written in one place. You can print or save this as a PDF.

Then click **Next** to move on to TORCH, where you'll use the AI tutor to explore your enquiry questions in depth.

> **Remember:** MAP saves automatically as you type. You can come back and edit it at any time.
