# Student Guide — The TORCH Stage

TORCH is where your investigation actually happens. You're not just reading and copying — you're thinking out loud with an AI tutor that pushes you to understand, not just collect. The AI won't write anything for you, but it will ask you questions that help you think more clearly.

---

## Before you start

Your planned questions from MAP appear at the top of the page as soon as you enter TORCH. These are the enquiry questions you wrote in your planning. You don't need to retype them — just click **Ask** next to any question and it drops straight into the box. Once you've asked it, it disappears from the list.

If you didn't write enquiry questions in MAP, go back and do that first. TORCH works best when you arrive with a plan.

---

## Step 1 — Set your goal

Before you type anything, pick a goal from the six options. Your goal tells the AI what kind of help you need right now:

| Goal | When to use it |
|---|---|
| **Knowledge** | You need a definition, a fact, or a key term explaining |
| **Understand** | You've read something but don't fully get it — you want it clearer |
| **Apply** | You want to use what you've learned in an example or context |
| **Analyse** | You want to break something apart — find causes, patterns, or connections |
| **Evaluate** | You want to weigh up evidence, make a judgement, or assess an argument |
| **Create** | You're planning, drafting, or designing something original |

You can change your goal as the conversation moves on. Start with Knowledge if you're building a base; try to push toward Analyse or Evaluate as you go deeper.

> *Tip: Your teacher has set target Bloom's levels for this task — you can see them in the task panel. If Evaluate is a target, make sure you spend some time in that goal, not just Knowledge.*

---

## Step 2 — Ask your question

Once you've set your goal, type your question in the box and click **Investigate** (or press Enter).

You can use one of your planned MAP questions by clicking **Ask** — that fills the box for you. Or type your own question from scratch.

**What makes a good question?**
- Specific beats vague: "What are the three factors that limit the rate of photosynthesis?" is better than "Tell me about photosynthesis."
- Questions that start with *Why*, *How*, *What is the difference between*, or *To what extent* tend to get the most useful responses.
- Ask one thing at a time. Don't pile multiple questions into one message.

---

## Step 3 — Read the response carefully

The AI will give you an explanation, an example, or a guiding question — depending on what you asked and what goal you selected. At the end of every response you'll see:

**Reflection Prompt:** — this is a question designed to push your thinking one step further. Don't ignore it. The Reflection Prompt gets harder as the conversation goes on — early ones ask you to recall and clarify; later ones ask you to evaluate and take a position.

Read the whole response before you type your next message. The AI has read everything you've said so far — including your MAP planning — so its questions are specific to your thinking, not generic.

---

## Step 4 — Keep the conversation going

One exchange isn't enough. A good TORCH investigation is a back-and-forth. After you read the response:

- **Answer the Reflection Prompt.** Even if you're not sure, try. The AI will work with what you give it.
- **Ask a follow-up.** If the response opens up a new question for you, ask it.
- **Change your goal.** If you started on Knowledge and now want to evaluate something, switch to Evaluate.

> *Tip: The sidebar on the right shows a breakdown of your question types as you go — Factual, Conceptual, Analytical, Evaluative. If you've only asked factual questions after 10 messages, that's a signal to push deeper.*

---

## What the AI will and won't do

**It will:**
- Explain concepts, define terms, give examples
- Suggest sources to read (textbooks, websites, databases) — follow those up in your RESEARCH stage
- Ask you questions that guide you toward higher-order thinking
- Reference the goals, success criteria, and key vocabulary your teacher set

**It won't:**
- Write your essay or final answer
- Give you a list of points to copy
- Tell you what conclusion to reach

If it seems like the AI is being evasive when you want a direct answer — that's intentional. Try rephrasing as a knowledge question ("What is the definition of X?") to get a factual explanation first.

---

## The Notes panel

On the right side of the screen there's a **My Notes** box. Use it to jot down anything useful as you investigate — initial hypotheses, things you want to come back to, ideas that strike you. These notes carry through to the Final Response stage, so you can refer back to them when you're writing up.

---

## When to move on

You're ready to leave TORCH when:
- You've asked most of your MAP enquiry questions
- You can explain the key ideas in your own words
- You have a starting position or argument forming in your head
- You know what sources you want to look at in the RESEARCH stage

There's no minimum number of messages, but one or two exchanges is usually not enough to really investigate a complex question. Aim for a real conversation — at least five or six turns.

> **Remember:** TORCH saves automatically. You can come back to it at any time, even after moving to later stages.
