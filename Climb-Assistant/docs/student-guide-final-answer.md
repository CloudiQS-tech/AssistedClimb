# Student Guide — The Final Answer Stage

This is where everything comes together. You've planned in MAP, investigated in TORCH, logged your sources in RESEARCH, and checked your thinking in COMPASS. Now you write your answer — properly, in your own words, from your own understanding.

---

## Before you start writing

Read the task question again at the top of the page. Not quickly — properly. Make sure you're answering the actual question, not a version of it you've constructed in your head over the course of the climb.

Check the notes panel on the right. It shows your notes from TORCH and your key findings from RESEARCH. Use them as reference — you don't need to remember everything, but you should understand it well enough to write about it without copying it verbatim.

---

## Writing your answer

**Write from your own understanding, not from your sources.**

Your answer should be in your own words. Pasting is disabled — this is intentional. An answer you've typed yourself, even if it's rougher, is worth more than a polished paragraph you've copied and slightly changed. Your teacher is assessing your thinking, not your ability to find and rearrange other people's sentences.

**Answer the question directly.**

Start by making your position or argument clear. Don't spend three paragraphs building up to the point — state it, then support it with evidence and reasoning.

**Use your key vocabulary.**

Your teacher has set key terms for this task. Use them — correctly, in context, not just dropped in to look like you know them. If you're not sure whether you're using a term correctly, think about how it was explained in TORCH before using it.

**Draw on more than one source.**

A strong answer doesn't rely on a single piece of evidence. Use findings from different sources, especially where they agree or where one challenges another. Show that your conclusion holds up across the evidence, not just from one place.

**Acknowledge complexity.**

If the question has a genuine counterargument, acknowledge it in your answer. You don't have to agree with it — but dismissing it entirely or pretending it doesn't exist makes your answer weaker, not stronger.

## Checking your answer with AI

Once you've written a solid draft, you can use **Check my answer** to get AI feedback. This cross-references your answer against:

- The task question and success criteria
- Key vocabulary your teacher expects
- Expected length or format (if your teacher set one)

The feedback comes back in four sections:

- **What you've covered** — specific things you've done well
- **What's missing or underdeveloped** — named gaps or unmet criteria
- **Vocabulary check** — which key terms you've used, which are missing
- **Next steps** — concrete things to add or improve

**You have two checks.** Use the first one when you have a solid draft — not a rough first attempt. Read the feedback carefully, make your improvements, then use the second check on your revised version. After that, the button locks and the answer is yours to submit as-is.

> *The AI feedback is a guide, not a grade. It doesn't know everything your teacher values. Use it to catch obvious gaps — don't treat it as the final word.*

---

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Writing too generally.** "Climate change has many effects on the environment" tells the reader nothing. "Rising sea temperatures have caused coral bleaching across 50% of the Great Barrier Reef since 2016" is specific, evidenced, and useful.

**Only using one perspective.** If every point in your answer comes from the same angle, you haven't really engaged with the question — you've just repeated one position at length.

**Copying from your research notes.** Your notes are a starting point, not a script. Paraphrase, synthesise, and connect — don't transcribe.

**Answering a different question.** Re-read what you've written and ask: does every sentence actually address the task question? If a sentence doesn't, cut it or redirect it.

**Saving only at the end.** Your answer saves automatically as you type — but don't leave the page mid-draft without finishing your thought.

---

## When you're done

There's no submit button — your teacher can see your work and will review it directly. Once you're happy with your answer and have used your AI checks wisely, you're finished with the Climb.

> **Your answer is yours.** Every word should reflect your thinking — the investigation you did, the sources you evaluated, the argument you built. That's what the whole Climb has been for.
