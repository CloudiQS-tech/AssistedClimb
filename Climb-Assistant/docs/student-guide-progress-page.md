# Your Progress Page — Student Guide

When you click **Progress** in the navigation bar at the top of your screen, you'll see an overview of your whole Climb in one place. Here's what everything means.

---

## The stage cards

You'll see five cards across the page — one for each stage of the Climb:

| Stage | What it's for |
|---|---|
| MAP | Planning and framing your thinking before you begin |
| TORCH | Using the AI tutor to explore and investigate |
| COMPASS | Stepping back to check your thinking |
| Final Response | Writing your answer and reflecting on how you got there |
| RESEARCH | Recording and evaluating the sources you used |

Each card shows:

- **A status** — *Not started*, *In progress*, or *Complete*
- **A count** — for example, "3/5 questions" or "7 messages" — so you can see how much you've done
- **A progress bar** — a quick visual of how far through that stage you are
- **A Start or Continue button** — click it to jump straight to that stage

---

## What the colours mean

- **Green** — you've completed that stage
- **Amber** — you've started but haven't finished yet
- **Grey** — you haven't started this stage

---

## Teacher feedback

If your teacher has left feedback on your work, it will appear below your stage cards.

**Mid Point Formative Assessment** shows comments your teacher has written during the Climb — this is feedback to help you while you're still working, not a final grade.

**Your teacher's final assessment** appears once your teacher has reviewed your completed Climb. This shows your overall grade and their closing comments on your work.

---

## Tips

- Use the Progress page to check where you've got to before you start a session — it gives you a quick overview without having to click through each stage.
- If your teacher has left feedback, read it before you continue — it's there to help you improve your next stage.
- The progress bar shows *quantity* — how much you've filled in — not quality. Completing all the boxes doesn't mean the thinking inside them is finished. Always re-read what you've written and ask yourself: *does this actually show my thinking?*
