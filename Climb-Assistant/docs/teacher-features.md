# The Assisted Climb — Teacher Features

> **About this version:** This document covers **e-Assisted Climb** — the electronic version of The Assisted Climb. The "e" reflects the digital, platform-based delivery of the framework. Where The Assisted Climb is the wider pedagogical approach, e-Assisted Climb is the electronic tool that brings it to life: the structured student workspace, the Socratic AI tutor, and the teacher intelligence dashboard described throughout this document.

---

## How what a teacher sets up shapes what students experience

Everything the teacher puts into the task becomes the backbone of the student's entire Climb. It's not just a brief they read once — it's woven into every stage:

- The **task question** stays visible on every single page, so students are never lost about what they're working towards.
- The **key vocabulary** the teacher enters gets fed to the AI tutor — so when a student is chatting in the TORCH stage, the AI will naturally nudge them towards using those specific words, reinforcing the language the teacher wants them to use.
- The **success criteria** sit alongside the student's work as a self-check — they can measure themselves against the teacher's own standard before submitting anything.
- The **stage instructions** mean the teacher can write specific guidance for each stage — so MAP might say "focus on what you already know", while COMPASS might say "think about whether your sources agree with each other."
- The teacher can also **switch off stages entirely** — so if a particular task doesn't need a Research stage, students won't see it. The Climb adapts to what the teacher has designed.

## Setting a task — full options

Each class has one active task at a time. When creating or editing a task, the teacher can set:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| Task title & question | Shown to students on every page of their workspace |
| Success criteria | Students self-check their work against these before finishing |
| Key vocabulary | The AI tutor nudges students to use these words during TORCH |
| Bloom's Taxonomy levels | Sets the cognitive depth expected (Remember → Create) |
| Stage instructions | Custom guidance shown at the start of each stage |
| Expected length | Tells students how much is expected in their final response |
| Resource links | Optional reference materials linked for students |
| Active stages | Switch individual stages on or off for this task |

## What the teacher sees as students work

The teacher has a live view of every student in the class. At a glance they can see which stage each student is at — not started, in progress, or finished — across every stage. This means they can spot who's stuck before the lesson ends.

Clicking into any student shows the teacher everything that student has written:

- **MAP** — what the student said they already know, where they think their gaps are, and the enquiry questions they came up with.
- **TORCH** — the full back-and-forth conversation with the AI tutor. Every question the student asked, every response. The teacher can see exactly how a student is thinking, not just what they eventually wrote.
- **RESEARCH** — every source the student found, what they took from it, and how they assessed its reliability.
- **COMPASS** — the student's verification and reflection on what they found.
- **Final Response (EMBED)** — the student's finished written answer.

## Teaching Intelligence — Class Insights

This is where it gets genuinely powerful at a class level. The teacher clicks into Teaching Intelligence for a class and gets a real-time picture of how the whole group is doing.

The platform automatically analyses every question every student asked the AI tutor during TORCH and classifies them — Factual, Conceptual, Analytical, Evaluative, Creative. The teacher gets a breakdown showing, for example, that 60% of questions were factual and only 8% were evaluative. That tells a teacher something important: students are mostly asking "what is" rather than "why does this matter" — and they can adjust accordingly.

The teacher can then press one button to generate an AI analysis of the whole class, which produces:

- **What students seem confident about** — areas where the class showed strong understanding.
- **Where gaps appear** — topics or concepts that came up repeatedly as stumbling blocks.
- **Actual misconceptions, word for word** — direct quotes from student work showing exactly where the thinking went wrong.
- **Stage completion patterns** — for example, most students completed MAP but dropped off at COMPASS.
- **Teaching recommendations** — specific suggestions for what to address in the next lesson.
- **A suggested next lesson plan** — a ready-made starting point based on what this class actually struggled with.

All of this can be copied to the clipboard or printed as a PDF — so it can go straight into a teacher's planning notes or be shared with a department head.

## Giving feedback on individual students

For each student, the teacher can leave two kinds of feedback at every stage of the Climb:

**Formative feedback** — real-time, in-progress comments the student sees immediately while they're still working. Designed to feel like personal coaching: "You're asking great questions — now push yourself to evaluate the sources."

**Summative feedback** — a final assessment once the work is done. The teacher picks an attainment level (Emerging, Developing, Secure, Exceeding) and writes a closing comment for that stage.

### AI-assisted marking — with teacher review built in

To save time, the teacher can ask the AI to draft all formative comments in one go, or all summative grades and comments in one go — across every stage for that student.

Crucially, **nothing is saved until the teacher confirms it.** The process works like this:

1. The teacher clicks **Generate Formative Feedback** or **Generate Summative Feedback.**
2. The AI drafts fill into the text boxes for every stage — but they are held as a draft only.
3. A banner appears at the top of the page: *"AI-generated feedback is ready — review and edit below, then submit."* This banner stays visible no matter how far the teacher scrolls.
4. The teacher reads through every stage, edits any comment or grade they want to adjust, and makes the feedback their own.
5. When satisfied, they click **Submit Feedback** — only then is anything saved to the student's record.
6. If they want to start again, **Discard** returns every box to exactly what it was before the AI ran.

This means the teacher is always in control. The AI accelerates the process — it never replaces the teacher's professional judgement.

### Overall assessment

Once individual stage feedback is complete, the teacher can generate an **overall assessment** — a holistic summary of how the student performed across the whole Climb. This also follows the review-and-submit process before being saved.

### Student reports

Once feedback is complete, the teacher can print or export a PDF report for any individual student, summarising their work and all feedback across every stage of the Climb.
